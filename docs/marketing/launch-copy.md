### Variant A — the contrarian build
**Title:** Eduyro - an edtech app where the product is a piece of paper

Everyone building edtech (me included, at first) assumes the deliverable is a
screen. I built Eduyro to end at the printer instead: an adaptive engine places
a child in math, reading, writing and science, then generates a personalized
printable worksheet every day at their placed levels. The child never touches
the app. Advancement is gated on 90% mastery; problem sets regenerate
infinitely so no two sheets repeat.

Why paper: the parents I talked to didn't want another app to police — they
wanted 20 quiet minutes at the kitchen table. Retention problem becomes a
printing habit, which is a much older and stickier technology.

Stack: Next.js, Postgres, PDF generation pipeline. Solo. $9.99/mo, 7-day trial
with no card required. https://www.eduyro.com — would love feedback on the
sample worksheet on the homepage.

### Variant C — the lessons-learned post
**Title:** Eduyro - what I learned building an adaptive-learning app parents run from a printer

Shipped: Eduyro, a 4-subject (math/reading/writing/science) adaptive program
whose daily output is a printable worksheet. Three lessons from building it
solo:

1. Parents don't trust "adaptive" until they see the placement result. So the
   placement report shows the level per subject with what was tested — and the
   trial (7 days, no card) is basically "run the placement, print a week,
   decide."
2. Mastery gates need teeth or they're theatre. Eduyro requires 90% before a
   level advances. Parents in testing liked being able to point at the number.
3. Unlimited regeneration beat every "content library" feature I planned.
   Fresh problems for the same skill, every print, with answer keys.

$9.99/mo all four subjects, packs from $4.99 one-time.
https://www.eduyro.com — the homepage has a real sample worksheet; I'd
genuinely value critique of the sheet layout from anyone who's done print CSS.
